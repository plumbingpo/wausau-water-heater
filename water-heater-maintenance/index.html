If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Wausau, WI, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually setting off error codes. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. You get a straightforward report after every visit, noting what is healthy and what needs attention. Call (715) 201-5024 to book maintenance for your Wausau water heater.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest is harder than online videos make it seem. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not something to guess about.

We come prepared for what an older heater throws at us: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. What you get is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
