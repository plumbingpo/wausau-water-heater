A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Wausau, WI, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Wausau Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Wausau call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. A wrong guess there just wastes money. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

What a trained technician brings is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. Before we leave, every connection is leak-tested, the vent is confirmed to carry exhaust outside, and CO is checked. And because some gas heaters are not worth another repair, we give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement first, then handle either one with correct venting, gas piping, and the code items your installation requires.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
