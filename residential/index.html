Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

No homeowner likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Wausau Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly corrodes away, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. Catching it early often keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The date code on the label usually gives it away. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Each maintenance visit from Wausau Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and confirms a safe water temperature. Minor faults are caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your water heater stays running at full efficiency.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank needs a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water calls for regular descaling to protect its heat exchanger working, and a large family cycles the heater far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every service keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Think of maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Getting the size right matters more than most homeowners realize. Before quoting a new heater, Wausau Water Heater works through the details that drive your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, showers that run back to back, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and your incoming water temperature. The result is a heater with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to handle your busiest hour without paying to heat water you'll never use.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than the number of gallons. We'll compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Wausau

Homes in Wausau run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Wausau Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
